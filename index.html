Saving

The game state is plain data kept apart from the renderer, so a save is just the seed plus what the simulation has changed — road wear, towns and who's currently walking. Autosaves every 30 seconds. A share code is the same thing as text: paste one into another browser and it continues from exactly there.

What to watch for

  • Nothing is placed. No road, junction or town is authored — all of it is downstream of where the ground is cheap to cross.
  • Rivers and mountains push back. Wading a river or climbing a ridge is expensive, so traffic funnels through fords and passes.
  • Wear compounds. Walked ground is easier to walk, so the first faint tracks pull in the next travellers and thicken into roads.
  • Crossroads settle. A caravan carrying enough people weighs up every town it could join against the best empty junction on the map, and sometimes stops for good — that is where towns come from.
  • Housing decides who stays. Towns build homes when they run short of beds, and a town with free beds is the one passing caravans choose.
  • The first houses are the wagons. A founding party unhitches its covered wagons and pitches them as tents; timber houses replace them one at a time, on the same ground, as the town gets round to felling enough wood.
  • Buildings are made of somewhere. Timber comes off the forest, stone out of a quarry that has to be dug first, and nothing made of masonry — the well, the market, the smithy — can be started until it is. A town with no rock within reach has to trade for its stone or stay a village of houses.
  • Everybody eats. Hunting and fishing are free but the land caps them, so a growing town has to clear fields — and clearing costs it the hands that were feeding people. A town that outruns its food stops growing, and one that runs out sends its people away to find somewhere better.
  • The borders go quiet. Arrivals from off the map taper off; before long most traffic is towns sending their own surplus somewhere else.
  • A ford that gets busy enough becomes a bridge.
